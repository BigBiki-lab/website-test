How can you create a magnetic field around a wire?

pass a current through it

Where is the magnetic field of a wire strongest?

close to the wire

How can you increase the strength of the field around the wire?

increase the current

How can you change the direction of the field around the wire?

change the direction of the current

Motor effect

A wire carrying a current experiences a force when it is placed between two magnets.
The current ia the wire creates a magnetic field around the wire and this interacts with the magnetic field between the magnets.
The force is gratest when the wire is at right angles to the magnetic field produced by the magnets, and is zero when the wire is in the same direction as this magnetic field.
There is an equal an opposite force on the magnets.

Electric motors

Diagram shows a simplified motor (a real motor would have many more turns of wire on the coil). There is a force on each part of the wire carrying a current in the magnetic field and so using a coil with many turns of wire increases the total force turning the coil.
The split-ring commutator ensures that the force on the coil always turns it in the same direction. In diagram, the part of the coil labelled X is moving upwards. If the current in this part of the wire was still moving in the same direction (towards us on the diagram) half a turn later, the force on it would still be upwards, and this would have the effect of trying to turn the coil in the opposite direction. The commutator ensures that the current is always flowing in the correct direction to make the coil continue to spin.
